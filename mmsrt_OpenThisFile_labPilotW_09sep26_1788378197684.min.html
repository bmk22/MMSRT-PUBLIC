Cadence:

RT by sequence position (1–8), learned-sequence (phase L) trials only, using only each round's last min(4, reps) repetitions and excluding misses/away-flagged trials. Pick 1-Cadence Sets (Legacy A/B, Progression-Generalization C/D, plus their Monotonic/Silent controls — single authentic cadence highlighted at position 5) or 2-Cadence Sets (wx W/X, uv U/V, plus their own Monotonic2C/silent2C controls — two group cadences highlighted at positions 4 and 8) above; only one is plotted at a time. Each box is the distribution of per-subject mean RT at that position/run/condition combination — exactly one dot per subject (a subject contributing more than one matching round to the same combination has those rounds' means averaged together first, rather than plotting one dot per round) — split further by run if “All runs” is selected. Requires a seqIdx field on trials; files/rounds/trials that predate it are silently skipped rather than plotted as zero.

Diagnostic overview, not tied to the condition/subject/run filters above: one horizontal box plot per batch currently loaded, showing how many total runs each subject in that batch has completed (x-axis), with every subject plotted as its own jittered dot. Useful for spotting batches with unusually high dropout (most dots clustered near a low run count) or unusually high engagement (dots spread out toward high run counts, likely from infinity mode).

How to Win Over Fans

Before you start preparing for the first leg of your tour you will complete a short practice jam to confirm you understand the task. Visitors will wander in and your objective is to win them over by playing what they like to hear. Watch the speech bubble above each visitor for clues about each one’s unique preference. When a speech bubble turns green it means you've won them over, yellow means you're getting warmer, and red means not yet.

To proceed, you must satisfy 3 different visitors, each within the time limit. You’ll need to maintain each visitors preferred pattern and tempo for a few seconds before they’ll be satisfied, and you’ll need to do it within the time limit.

You’ll use the same four keys you used at your factory job: H, J, K, L. You can satisfy visitors using any keys in any order. During the practice jams pressing a key will always produce a sound.
